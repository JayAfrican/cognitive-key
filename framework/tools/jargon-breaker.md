# The Jargon Breaker

Nine operations that take a surface and strip it back to what it is hiding. Run on press releases, policy language, earnings calls, layoff memos, terms of service, consultant decks, political statements — anything that sounds authoritative but will not say who did what to whom.

**Verdicts are about language. Never about people.**

---

## The core instruction: posit, don't interrogate

**Do not hand back a list of questions. Work out what the phrase most likely meant — from the definitions of the words actually in it — and say so.**

This is not a stylistic preference. **A leading question is the exact structure this tool exists to catch.** *"Carried back to whom?"* insinuates concealment while asserting nothing — the asker owns none of it and can deny it afterwards. That is J9's alternative-set move, performed by the analyst instead of the target. **An analyst who only asks questions is committing the sin they were hired to find.**

So every operation that fires produces **a posited plain reading**, stated as a claim, carrying:

- **the sense you fixed** — when a key word has more than one, name the one you are using and why (usually: the one the sentence needs to be true)
- **a confidence, 0–100**
- **what would show it wrong** — one checkable thing

Below 60, say so and give the runner-up reading. **You are allowed to be wrong out loud. You are not allowed to imply without committing.**

---

## Two gates

**1 · The disclosure test.** Would the move still work if you narrated it aloud? *"That word is built from 'carry back', so I read this as the decision being handed to someone else"* is **improved** by being said out loud — it hands the other person the same instrument. If narrating a move would break it, that move is influence, not analysis. Drop it.

**2 · The default reading is "they meant the ordinary thing."** Most opacity is habit, not strategy. A tool that finds manipulation in every sentence is not screening anything — it is reading English. **Expect most passages to come back HABIT, and always report the operations that found nothing.**

---

## The nine operations

Run in order. Skip what does not fire; name what you skipped.

**J1 · Denominalize — restore the verb, restore the actor.** Turning a verb into a noun deletes the person doing it. *"A restructuring"* has no subject. **Posit the missing subject and object from context:** *"The board is cutting roles in the division and has not said which."*

**J2 · Restore the passive.** *"Mistakes were made."* **Name who most plausibly made them**, with a confidence. Passive voice and nominalisation are the same deletion by two routes.

**J3 · Residual screen.** *Non-core assets. Non-performing loans. Underserved. Non-traditional. Legacy systems.* A term defined by what it is **not** names a category whose contents share only the instrument that missed them, and it often does moral work while looking technical. **Posit what is actually in the bin**, then state the positive criterion the writer could have used instead.

**J4 · One word, two jobs.** *Efficiency* means both *same output from fewer inputs* and *less output from far fewer inputs*. Same for **reform, flexibility, engagement, value, growth, alignment, resources, savings.** Name both senses, **commit to the one the sentence needs to be true**, and say which one the audience will hear — the gap between those two is often the whole finding.

**J5 · Find the concrete referent.** *Synergies* — which ones. *Operational footprint* — which buildings, which people. **Posit the referent.** If it genuinely cannot be posited even loosely, **that is the finding**, and state it as one rather than as a question.

**J6 · Cui bono, asked about the sentence.** Not *"is this person bad."* **Whose interest does this phrasing serve, and who would phrase it differently?** Answer both halves — write out the sentence the other party would have written.

**J7 · The plain restatement test.** Rewrite in one sentence a twelve-year-old could act on. Then: **is the plain version uncomfortable in a way the original is not?** If yes, the jargon was not imprecision — it was precision aimed at a different goal. If no, it was habit. **Say which.**

**J8 · What is being carried, by whom, in what direction.** English's vocabulary for handling ideas and running institutions is built on one template: a preposition welded to a body-scale physical verb.

| Root | Physical verb | Built |
|---|---|---|
| \*bher- | carry | in·fer, re·fer, con·fer, trans·fer, de·fer, dif·fer |
| \*deuk- | lead | in·duce, de·duce, re·duce, con·duct, pro·duce, ab·duct |
| \*kap- | grasp | ac·cept, ex·cept, re·ceive, con·ceive, per·ceive, inter·cept |
| \*sta- | stand | in·sist, con·sist, re·sist, per·sist, ex·ist, sub·stance |

*"We defer to the committee"* → carried **away**. Posited reading: **the committee holds this now and we do not, and that transfer is what the sentence is for.** *"Costs will be transferred"* → carried **across**: posit which balance sheet to which, and say how confident you are.

**The leash, and J8 needs the tightest one because it is the most fun.** The construction licenses a reading **of the sentence**. It is not evidence about what the speaker was thinking, and *refer* does not secretly mean *carry back* — it means refer. Do not claim speakers unconsciously simulate the physical act: that is embodied semantics, and the flagship paradigms failed large preregistered multi-lab replications (Morey et al. 2022). **A technique that never returns "nothing here" is not screening anything — so on a clean Latinate abstraction, say plainly that the construction revealed nothing.**

**J9 · Where is the stress, and what is it contrasting with?** *(spoken sources only)* *"We* ***reviewed*** *the proposal"* implies *and did not approve it* — asserted by nobody, carried entirely by pitch, deniable by design. Under Rooth's alternative semantics (1992), stressing a word evokes the set of alternatives that could have filled the slot; the set is never spoken, so the listener builds it and the speaker owns none of it.

1. Locate the stress — usually gone from a transcript. Recover it from audio, or say you cannot.
2. Name the alternative set: *reviewed, as opposed to what?*
3. **State the implicature as a flat sentence.**
4. **Then answer: would the speaker assert that sentence on the record?** If yes — ordinary emphasis, no finding. If no — the stress did work the speaker declined to sign.

**Counterweight: focus is universal grammar, not a technique.** Every language with intonation marks contrast by prominence, and signed languages mark it by other means. **The exploitation is a special case of a universal, so J9's default output is "ordinary emphasis."**

## Output

**1 · The table** — one row per operation that fired:

| Phrase | Op | Sense fixed | **Posited reading** | Conf. | What would show it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**2 · The plain version** — the whole passage rewritten in plain English, as you believe it was meant. One paragraph, no hedging inside it; the hedges live in the confidence column above. **This is the deliverable.**

**3 · The verdict on the passage** — exactly one, about the language and not the speaker:

- **HABIT** — opaque, but the plain version is equally comfortable. No finding.
- **IMPRECISION** — an actor or referent is genuinely missing, probably by accident. Name what to ask for.
- **AIMED** — the plain version is uncomfortable in a way the original is not. **The opacity is doing work.** State what work, and for whom.

**4 · The null line** — which operations found nothing. A screen that never reports a null is not screening.

## Sourcing discipline

**Never cite NLP.** Cite the mechanism and its actual source: Rooth (1992) for focus and alternative sets; Orwell, *Politics and the English Language* (1946) for plain restatement. The single exception is J1 — denominalisation is the NLP Meta Model's core move and gets a credit line there, as a credit and not an authority.

**No etymological claim leaves the output without the lookup recorded at the point of use** — named in the text, not a footnote.
