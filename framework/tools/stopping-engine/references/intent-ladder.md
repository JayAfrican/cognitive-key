# Stopping Engine — the intent ladder

> Source: `stopping-engine.md` (v1, 2026-09-06; §6a added 2026-09-23). Copied verbatim for the plugin; the project document stays the master copy. If they disagree, the project document wins and this file gets refreshed.

## 6a · The intent ladder — **five tiers, and only the top one is conditioned** *(added 2026-09-23, JayArr's challenge)*

**Courts determine intent every day.** *Mens rea* is the foundation of criminal law; scienter is an element of fraud; discrimination law separates **disparate treatment** (intent) from **disparate impact** (effect) precisely because both are findable. **A blanket "intent is unknowable" is not a cautious position. It is a wrong one**, and it hands every party that benefits from an arrangement a permanent defence the instrument invented for them.

**What courts actually have is not better insight. It is a procedure:** a stated standard of proof · named parties · rules of evidence · compelled production · **the accused present and answering** · a decision-maker with jurisdiction · appeal.

> **So the constraint is not *no intent verdicts*. It is *no intent verdict without the procedure that makes one legitimate* — and the tiers below say what each rung costs.**

| Tier | Finding | What it requires | Permitted output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I0** | **Effect** | The outcome, measured | *The system produces X.* No intent claim. **Always admissible** |
| **I1** | **Foreseeability** | The information existed and was reachable by the party, at a stated date | ***Knew or should have known.*** Constructive knowledge — a real legal standard, and no mind-reading |
| **I2** | **Notice** | A document: memo, report, filed comment, minutes, correspondence | ***On notice from [date].*** Still no claim about what anyone wanted |
| **I3** | **Declination** | The proposal **and** the refusal, with the grounds as stated at the time | ***A remedy was available on [date] and was declined on [stated grounds].*** **An act, not a motive** |
| **I4** | **Intent** | The named party given the material **and a right of reply** — or a finding already made by a body that has that procedure (court, regulator, statutory inquiry) | *The outcome was sought.* **Conditioned, never forbidden** |

### 6a-i · Why this is the corpus's own principle rather than a concession

**`session-brief.md` §0: the person holds the pen.** The whole apparatus exists because descriptions were made of one person that he had no standing to answer. **`output-standing-rules.md` §14b** closes employer-facing products on the identical ground — *used on people, by parties with power over them.*

> **An unanswerable verdict about a person is the thing this project was built against. The bar at I4 is not scepticism about intent. It is the appeal, applied to the instrument's own output.**
>
> **A court may find malice because the person can argue back. This instrument may not, because nobody it names gets a turn** — unless the procedure is built, and I4 says what building it means.

### 6a-ii · What the ladder buys, and it is most of what was being argued for

**I0–I3 are not mind-claims and carry no §6 exposure at all.** They are enough for nearly every case this corpus has met:

- **Michigan is an I3 finding.** A remedy demonstrated to cut harm *and* cost, published, available — and largely not adopted. **Declining is an act with a date and a decision-maker.**
- **Tyack's administrative progressives are an I2/I3 finding** on the historical record, not a claim about anyone's soul.
- **The school board that did not fund an outcome check is I3** the moment the proposal and the refusal are in the minutes.

> ### **Malice is deniable. A declination on a stated date is not.**
>
> **I3 is the strongest rung that needs no one's permission, and it is harder to argue with than I4 would be.**

### 6a-iii · Two cautions, because the ladder can be misused in both directions

**1 · Do not use profiling as the model.** Behavioural profiling of the FBI kind has a **weak validation record** — comparisons of trained profilers against untrained comparison groups have repeatedly failed to show a reliable accuracy advantage. **Courts are the right analogy; profilers are not**, and citing them weakens a case that stands perfectly well on the legal one. *(Unverified this session — flagged, not asserted.)*

**2 · Tier inflation is the failure mode to watch.** The pull is to write I4 and support it at I2. **Every intent finding states its tier and its evidence in the same sentence, or it is not a finding.** A claim at I3 that reads like I4 has committed the error §4d-i was written to stop, one register up.

### 6a-iv · Self-investigation — admissible only where it cuts against the investigator *(added 2026-09-23)*

**When an institution investigates itself, its report is evidence from an interested party. It does not stop being evidence — it stops being neutral.** Split it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Statements against its own interest** — *we knew · we were told on this date · we declined the fix and here is why* | **Admissible, and usually the strongest material available.** No institution volunteers these |
| **Statements in its own favour** — *there was no conspiracy · no one acted in bad faith · this was a process failure* | **Not a finding. A position.** It may be true. It has no standing to establish itself |

> **The same bar applies to an acquittal as to a conviction.** *"Nobody was lying"* is an **I4 claim** and needs what any I4 claim needs. **An institution cannot supply it about itself.**

**And the reason this matters here:** a self-investigation that exonerates is **the self-protecting system producing its own verdict**, which is the thing under examination rather than a report on it.

### 6a-v · When does incompetence stop working as an explanation?

**The question is asked as *how many failures before it becomes malice*. That is the wrong shape, and the ladder makes it unnecessary.**

> **You never have to convert incompetence into malice. You have to find the document.**

**Incompetence is a claim about capacity** — *we did not know how.* It is testable: was the money there, the people, the information? **A decision is a different thing entirely, and the record tells them apart:**

- **No record of a choice** → incompetence remains available, and **I1** (*knew or should have known*) is the ceiling.
- **A proposal and a refusal with stated grounds** → **I3, and incompetence is off the table**, because declining a fix on cost is not a failure of capacity. It is a preference.

**GM is the worked case.** The 2005 memo closing the file — *none of the solutions represents an acceptable business case* — **settles it without anyone ruling on motive at all.** Repetition never did that work. **One document did.**

**Standing instruction:** *any output of this instrument that touches intent carries its tier label. An unlabelled intent claim is inadmissible regardless of how well supported it is.*
